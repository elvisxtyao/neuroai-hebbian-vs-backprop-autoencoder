## Scope and integrity

The formal robustness matrix is `BBB/HHH/HHB/HBB × seeds 0–4` with:

- Gaussian noise: `sigma = 0.0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4`;
- salt-and-pepper noise: `ratio = 0.0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4`;
- pixel masking: `ratio = 0.0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4`;
- fixed `noise_seed=2026`.

Severity zero is evaluated once and shared across all three curves. Each frozen
checkpoint therefore has 13 conditions, for 20 checkpoints and 260 formal
seed-level rows. Noise is generated by a stable counter hash of sample ID,
noise type, severity, stream, and pixel index. The first-batch tensor
fingerprint for every condition is identical across all methods and seeds.

The integrity gate reports:

- `20/20` frozen system/probe/standardized-decoder triples;
- `260/260` finite metric rows;
- all component checksums unchanged before/after evaluation;
- 13/13 deterministic noise fingerprints;
- no training and no performance gate;
- test data used only for post-freeze analysis, never selection.

Reconstruction is evaluated against the **clean image target**. It therefore
measures robust information recovery/denoising, not reproduction of the noisy
input.

## Interpreting representation cosine

HHH has the numerically highest clean–noisy z cosine (`≈0.9996–0.9999`) while
having the worst or near-worst noisy classification and absolute
reconstruction. This is not robust semantic representation. Q2 showed that
HHH z has effective rank near one. Perturbations can preserve the direction of
a collapsed representation while destroying its class-dependent magnitude or
probe predictions.

Consequently:

- representation cosine must be interpreted jointly with effective rank,
  prediction JS, classification, and reconstruction;
- HHH's near-one cosine is a collapse artifact, not evidence that biological
  local learning is more noise robust;
- a lower cosine in BBB/Hybrid models partly reflects that their
  higher-dimensional representations have more directions that can change.

## Q3 conclusions

1. **HBB is the strongest overall noisy classifier.** It has the highest mean
   accuracy at severity 0.4 for all three corruptions. Its advantage over HHB
   is especially large and paired-stable under Gaussian noise.
2. **HHB repairs clean classification but remains noise sensitive.** Its clean
   accuracy is highest, yet it degrades more than BBB/HBB under Gaussian and
   masking. Minimal BP Enc3 alone does not produce the robustness of BP Enc2
   plus Enc3.
3. **HHH is not robust despite its stable cosine.** It has the largest
   Gaussian degradation and worst absolute reconstruction at severe Gaussian
   and salt-and-pepper noise.
4. **Masking is less destructive than replacement/additive noise.** All methods
   retain over 80% accuracy at ratio 0.4, whereas Gaussian and
   salt-and-pepper produce much larger drops.
5. **System and standardized reconstruction agree on the main ordering.**
   HBB/BBB recover substantially more clean-image information than HHB/HHH
   under strong Gaussian and salt-and-pepper corruption. The dual decoder
   control therefore does not reduce this conclusion to decoder co-adaptation.

These conclusions apply to frozen clean-trained MNIST models. They do not
establish robustness to adversarial attacks, distribution shift, or
non-stationary training.
